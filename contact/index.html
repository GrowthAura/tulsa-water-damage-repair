What Should I Tell the Dispatcher?

The dispatcher uses the information you provide to send the right crew with the right equipment and to give you an accurate arrival window. Having these details ready makes the call faster and gets the crew to your door sooner.

Tell the dispatcher:

  • Your full address and zip code this determines which crew is closest and your arrival window
  • What happened burst pipe, appliance failure, storm damage, sewage backup, flooding, roof leak, or "I'm not sure but there's water"
  • When you noticed it this morning, last night, just now, or "it's been there for days"
  • Whether the water is still running and whether you've shut off the main valve
  • Any safety concerns water near electrical outlets or the panel, sagging ceilings, sewage smell, gas smell, structural damage

The dispatcher matches this information to a crew and equipment load. You get a realistic arrival window for your address before the call ends.

What Should I Do While I Wait for the Crew?

The steps you take between calling and the crew's arrival can save significant damage and strengthen your insurance claim. Every minute water sits, it spreads and absorbs deeper into materials.

  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe, fixture or appliance. The valve is usually near the water meter, in the garage or in a utility closet.
  • Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker only if you can reach the breaker panel without walking through standing water. If the panel is in a wet area, call the electric company.
  • Move electronics, documents, photos and valuables off wet floors and onto dry surfaces in dry rooms.
  • Photograph and video everything before moving anything wide angles of each room plus close-ups of the water source. This is your primary insurance evidence.
  • Place aluminum foil or plastic under furniture legs on wet carpet to prevent wood-stain transfer.
  • Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings water-loaded drywall can collapse without warning.
  • Don't use a household vacuum on standing water it's not designed for water and creates an electrical hazard.
  • Don't run the HVAC system if water may have reached ductwork or the air handler it can spread moisture and contaminants to dry rooms.

What Happens After I Call?

Dispatch: the nearest available crew is dispatched with extraction and drying equipment loaded. You receive an arrival window.

Arrival: the crew inspects the damage, confirms the water source is stopped, checks safety hazards, classifies the water and begins moisture mapping.

Extraction and drying: standing water is extracted, unsalvageable materials are removed, drying equipment is placed and readings are taken at every mapped point. The process is explained to you in plain language as it happens.
