What Are You Actually Dealing With After a House Fire?

A house fire produces four distinct types of damage, and all four need to be addressed often simultaneously for the home to be fully restored.

Burn damage
The areas directly touched by flame: charred framing, destroyed drywall, melted fixtures, consumed insulation and incinerated contents. These materials are structurally compromised and must be removed entirely.

Heat damage
Areas adjacent to the fire that weren't burned but were exposed to extreme heat: warped surfaces, melted plastics, blistered paint, discolored finishes and heat-damaged structural elements. Heat damage can extend well beyond the visible burn area.

Smoke and soot damage
The most widespread category. Smoke travels through ductwork, stairwells, wall cavities, chases and every gap in the building envelope reaching rooms that the fire never touched, sometimes on the opposite side of the home. Soot settles on every surface the smoke reached: ceilings, walls, floors, countertops, electronics, clothing, food and HVAC components.

Soot is acidic and begins a chemical reaction with surfaces immediately staining paint, etching glass, corroding metal and yellowing plastics. The longer soot sits, the harder it is to clean and the more damage it causes. Surfaces cleanable within the first 48 hours may be permanently damaged by day five. See

Fire suppression water damage
Firefighting hoses deliver enormous volumes of water in a short period, and fire sprinkler systems (in homes that have them) can discharge 15 to 25 gallons per minute per head. This water soaks floors, walls, ceilings and contents and once the fire is out, it sits in the structure. In Tulsa's warm, humid climate, that water can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours. Suppression water needs the same extraction, drying and monitoring as any water loss. See

How Do We Restore a Fire-Damaged Tulsa Home?

Fire restoration follows a strict sequence each step makes the next one possible, and doing them out of order wastes time and money.

1. Secure the property
As soon as the fire department releases the scene, we secure the building. Windows and doors damaged by the fire or by the fire department's entry are boarded up. Roof openings are tarped. Site fencing is installed if needed. The property must be weather-tight before interior work begins an open roof or broken window during Oklahoma's spring storms turns fire damage into fire-plus-flood damage.

2. Document everything before cleanup
Photos and a detailed inventory of both the structure and the contents are completed before any cleaning, removal or restoration begins. This documentation is the foundation of your fire insurance claim it establishes what was damaged, the condition of each item and the scope of the loss. Contents are categorized as restorable, questionable or non-restorable. See

3. Remove suppression water
Firefighting water is extracted from floors, walls and ceilings using the same truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment used for any water loss. Structural drying follows dehumidifiers, air movers, daily moisture readings at every mapped point. Drying the suppression water is time-critical because mold begins growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. See

4. Remove destroyed materials
Charred framing, destroyed drywall, melted insulation, burned flooring and heat-damaged structural elements are cut out, bagged and hauled away. Every removed item is photographed and documented for the insurance claim. Structural integrity is assessed damaged framing is evaluated for replacement.

5. Clean soot and smoke
Surfaces in every affected room are cleaned top-down using methods matched to the soot type dry-sponge techniques for powdery soot, degreasing solvents for oily soot, enzyme cleaners for protein residue. The HVAC system is assessed and ductwork cleaned or replaced as needed the HVAC system spreads soot and smoke to every room it serves. See

6. Remove embedded odor
Surface cleaning removes visible soot but not the smoke odor absorbed into porous materials insulation, framing, carpet pad, soft furnishings. Hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging treatments reach odor compounds embedded inside materials. For severe smoke penetration, some materials (insulation, carpet pad, heavily saturated drywall) need removal because the odor compounds are too deeply absorbed for any treatment to reach.

7. Clean or pack out contents
Restorable items hard surfaces, many furnishings, textiles, some electronics are cleaned on site or packed out to a specialized facility. Non-restorable items are documented with photos, descriptions and condition notes for the contents portion of the insurance claim.

8. Rebuild
New framing, drywall, insulation, flooring, trim and paint are installed after all cleaning, deodorization and drying are complete. Paint goes on last after deodorization because painting before odor treatment seals odor compounds behind the paint.

Why Does Fire Damage Need Fast Action?

Every hour that passes after a fire increases the scope and cost of restoration not from the fire itself (which is already out) but from the secondary damage that continues in its aftermath.

Soot is acidic and time-sensitive. The chemical reaction between soot and surfaces begins immediately and accelerates. Surfaces that could be cleaned and saved within 48 hours may be permanently damaged within a week turning cleaning jobs into replacement jobs.

Suppression water grows mold in 24-48 hours. Tulsa's warm, humid climate shortens the mold growth window. Firefighting water soaking into drywall, wood framing and insulation creates the same mold risk as any water loss and mold remediation on top of fire restoration adds significant cost and timeline. See

An open building invites more damage. Roof openings and broken windows let rain, wind, debris and animals into the structure. Board-up and tarping immediately after the fire department leaves prevents the loss from expanding.

How Much Does Fire Damage Restoration Cost in Tulsa?

Fire restoration costs vary enormously based on the fire's size, the extent of smoke and soot distribution, the amount of suppression water and the scope of the rebuild.

SituationTypical range
Kitchen fire, smoke in adjacent rooms, minor suppression waterFrom ~$5,000 to $15,000
Room fire with smoke through the whole houseFrom ~$15,000 to $40,000
Structure fire with heavy burn, smoke, soot, water and full rebuildFrom ~$40,000 to $150,000+

Fire insurance claims typically cover the full scope structure, contents, cleaning, deodorization, water drying and rebuild, plus additional living expenses if the home is uninhabitable during restoration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage in Oklahoma?

+
Fire is a standard covered peril under virtually all Oklahoma homeowners policies. Coverage typically includes the structure, contents, smoke and soot cleaning, firefighting water damage, deodorization, rebuild and additional living expenses (ALE) if the home is uninhabitable during restoration. Detailed contents inventories with photos, descriptions and approximate values speed the contents portion of the claim. See

Can I go back into my house after a fire?

+
Only after the fire department has cleared the structure for entry. Even then, keep children, elderly family members and anyone with respiratory conditions out of the home until soot cleanup, HEPA air filtration and deodorization are complete. Soot particles are fine enough to penetrate deep into the lungs.

What can be saved after a fire?

+
Often more than people expect. Hard, non-porous surfaces metal, glass, tile, sealed stone can usually be cleaned. Many furnishings, textiles and electronics can be restored through professional cleaning. Charred or heavily smoke-penetrated porous materials insulation, carpet pad, severely burned framing and drywall typically cannot be saved.

How long does fire restoration take?

+
A contained kitchen fire might be fully restored in 2 to 4 weeks. A room fire with whole-house smoke typically takes 4 to 8 weeks. A major structure fire requiring full rebuild can take 3 to 6 months or longer. Your project lead provides a timeline after the initial walkthrough.

Do you handle commercial fire damage?

+
Yes offices, retail, restaurants, warehouses and multi-family buildings. Commercial fire losses involve the same damage types plus business continuity, contents and inventory protection and multi-party insurance documentation. See
