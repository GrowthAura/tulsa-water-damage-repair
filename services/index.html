Mold, Fire & Storm

Water damage often comes with secondary problems. Mold starts growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. Storms that breach the roof also let in wind and debris. Fire suppression leaves water and soot damage together. These services address the secondary damage alongside the water loss.

  • Mold remediation find the moisture source, contain the area, remove contaminated materials, verify clean air
  • Storm damage repair emergency board-up and tarping, interior water extraction and drying from storm breaches
  • Fire damage restoration structural cleaning, odor removal and rebuild after fire and suppression water
  • Smoke and soot cleanup surface cleaning, air filtration and deodorization

How Mitigation and Restoration Work Together

Many homeowners aren't sure whether they need one service or two. Here's how the phases connect and why they're scoped separately.

Mitigation is the emergency phase. It starts within hours of the loss and typically runs 3 to 5 days. The goal is to stop the damage from growing: extract water, remove what can't be saved, dry the structure and document everything. Mitigation is billed based on documented equipment, labor and materials.

Restoration is the rebuild phase. It starts after drying is verified and can be scheduled at a pace that works for the homeowner. The goal is to put the home back together: drywall, insulation, flooring, trim and paint. Restoration is billed based on a line-item repair scope.

What Documentation Do You Provide for Insurance Claims?

Every job produces a complete documentation package built to the standards insurance adjusters use to review claims. Thorough documentation is the single most important factor in getting a claim approved at the correct amount and it's built into every project, not an add-on.

Your documentation includes:

  • Pre-mitigation photos the damage as found before any work begins
  • Moisture maps visual maps showing where moisture was detected, with readings at each point
  • Daily drying logs moisture readings at every mapped point, taken daily, showing the drying curve
  • Equipment logs what equipment was placed, where and for how long
  • Material removal documentation photos and notes for every material removed
  • Antimicrobial application records
  • Final dry verification readings confirming every material reached its dry target
  • Written mitigation scope an itemized scope your adjuster can review line by line
  • Written restoration scope a separate repair scope for the rebuild phase
