Which Commercial Properties Do We Handle?

We've dried every type of commercial property in the Tulsa metro. Each property type presents different challenges scale, contents, occupant coordination, business continuity requirements and insurance structures. Here's what we work on most often.

Offices
Downtown Tulsa high-rises, Midtown professional buildings and the 71st Street and Memorial Drive corridors. Office water damage typically involves server rooms, workstations, file storage and shared HVAC systems. A single line failure above a dropped ceiling can spread across an entire floor plate before it's discovered on a Monday morning.

Retail and restaurants
Strip centers and pad sites across Broken Arrow, Owasso , Bixby and Jenks. Retail losses involve inventory protection, customer-facing finishes and the pressure to reopen fast. Restaurants add food safety and health department concerns to the timeline.

Warehouses and industrial
Port of Catoosa area, Admiral Place, Sheridan Road and East Tulsa industrial corridors. Warehouse losses are about scale large floor plates, high ceilings, inventory staged on the floor. Sprinkler activations and roof leaks are the most common causes.

Multi-family
Apartments and condos where one unit's leak reaches three or four others below and beside it. Multi-unit losses require separate documentation for each unit, coordination with property management and per-unit tenant communication.

Specialty properties
Medical offices (with sterilization and HIPAA considerations), schools (with occupancy and air quality requirements), churches and hotels.

What Makes Commercial Water Damage Different from Residential?

Commercial water damage isn't just bigger residential water damage it involves different variables, different pressures and different documentation requirements.

Scale and complexity
A single failed line on an upper floor of a multi-story building can affect suites, tenants and building systems on every floor below it HVAC plenums, elevator shafts, fire alarm systems, data closets. Residential homes rarely have these interconnected systems.

Sprinkler activations
A single fire sprinkler head can discharge 15 to 25 gallons per minute. In the time it takes for someone to find the shut-off valve sometimes 20 to 30 minutes one head can release hundreds of gallons across a floor plate. Sprinkler water is often stagnant and classified as Category 2, requiring antimicrobial treatment of everything it contacts.

Contents at risk
Servers, network equipment, patient records, inventory, point-of-sale systems and specialized fixtures all need rapid protection or pack-out alongside the structural drying. The contents loss on a commercial job can exceed the structural loss.

Tenants and occupants
Property managers and building owners need clear, timely updates. Multi-tenant buildings need per-unit documentation. Tenants need to know which areas are safe, which are closed and how long the disruption will last.

Business continuity
Every closed hour has a revenue impact. Drying has to work around operating hours, customer access and tenant schedules. The loudest and most disruptive work extraction, selective demolition is scheduled after hours when possible. Unaffected areas are kept open when safe.

How Do We Run a Commercial Water Damage Job?

Our commercial process is structured around two priorities: dry the building fast and keep operations running wherever it's safe to do so.

Walkthrough and scope
A project lead walks every affected area and identifies at-risk building systems electrical panels, HVAC equipment, data closets, elevators, fire alarm systems. The drying plan, work zones, timeline and crew schedule are set with your facilities contact or property manager before equipment goes in.

Extraction at scale
Multiple truck-mounted and portable extraction units run simultaneously on large floor plates. Additional crews can run in parallel on multi-story or multi-wing losses. The goal is to get standing water off the floor as fast as possible to preserve contents, finishes and structural materials.

Drying around operations
Work zones are defined and physically separated from occupied areas. Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned to minimize noise, foot traffic and disruption to operating areas. When the loudest work has to happen in occupied space, it's scheduled after hours, on weekends or during planned closures. Drying follows IICRC S500 standards with daily moisture readings at every mapped point see .

Contents protection and pack-out
Contents are photographed, inventoried and either dried on site or packed out and stored off-site. Electronics are assessed and handled separately. The contents inventory supports the contents portion of your insurance claim.

Documentation for carriers and risk managers
Your documentation package includes:

  • Pre-mitigation photos of all affected areas
  • Moisture maps with readings at every point
  • Daily drying logs showing the drying curve
  • Equipment placement and runtime records
  • Contents inventory with condition notes and photos
  • Material removal documentation
  • Written mitigation scope, itemized for adjuster review
  • Separate written restoration scope for the rebuild

Mold prevention
Antimicrobial treatment on all affected structure. If growth is found common in concealed spaces like above drop ceilings and inside wall cavities our team handles containment and removal within the same project.

What Usually Causes Commercial Water Losses in Tulsa?

Understanding the most common causes helps property managers and facilities teams prioritize prevention and respond faster when losses occur.

  • Hail and wind damage to flat and low-slope roofs Tulsa's spring storm season hits commercial roofs hard. Flat and low-slope roofs are especially vulnerable to ponding water after hail damage.
  • Fire sprinkler activations and freeze breaks accidental sprinkler activations and freeze breaks in unheated mechanical areas during winter are among the most expensive single-event commercial losses
  • Rooftop HVAC condensate and chiller leaks large commercial HVAC units on the roof produce significant condensate that can overwhelm drain lines or leak through rooftop penetrations
  • Aging supply and drain lines older downtown and Midtown commercial buildings have supply and drain lines that corrode and fail with age, often inside walls and above ceilings where leaks run for days before discovery
  • Tenant-side failures restroom overflows, break-room appliance leaks and unauthorized plumbing modifications in tenant suites
  • River and stormwater flooding properties along the Arkansas River corridor and low-lying areas are exposed during major rain events.

+
Often, at least partially. We work in sections, and unaffected areas are kept open wherever safe. Disruptive work is scheduled after hours when possible. If an area must close for safety, contamination or drying effectiveness we explain the reason, the duration and any alternatives. The goal is always to minimize business interruption.

+
Yes we provide moisture maps, daily drying logs, equipment runtime records, contents inventories and an itemized scope formatted for your carrier or risk manager's review. We can meet your adjuster or third-party consultant on site and walk them through the documentation.
