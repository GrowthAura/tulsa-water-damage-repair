Why Do Pipes Burst in Tulsa?

Tulsa homes are engineered for the region's typically mild winters which means water lines in attics, garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls often have minimal insulation. When temperatures stay well below freezing for an extended period, the water inside those lines freezes, expands and splits the pipe or fitting.

The February 2021 winter storm demonstrated this vulnerability at scale. Temperatures across Green Country dropped below zero for multiple consecutive days far beyond what Tulsa's housing stock was built to handle. Thousands of pipes burst across Tulsa, Broken Arrow , Owasso , Jenks , Bixby and surrounding communities, overwhelming plumbers and restoration companies for weeks.

Beyond freezing, other causes of burst pipes in Tulsa include:

  • Corroded galvanized lines homes built before the 1970s in Midtown, North Tulsa, Brookside and older Claremore neighborhoods often still have galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside over decades, weakening the pipe wall until it fails
  • Failed fittings soldered copper joints and push-fit connections can fail from age, vibration or thermal cycling
  • Water hammer sudden pressure surges from fast-closing valves (washing machines, dishwashers) that stress joints and fittings over time
  • Polybutylene (PB) pipe homes built in the 1980s and early 1990s may have polybutylene supply lines, a material known for brittleness and failure at fittings

Frozen Pipe vs. Burst Pipe What's the Difference and What Should I Do?

These are two stages of the same problem, and the correct response differs for each.

Frozen pipe (no water coming out of a faucet):
The pipe is blocked by ice but hasn't split yet. Shut off the main water valve now before it thaws open the affected faucet and call a plumber. The plumber can thaw the line in a controlled way and inspect for damage. Never use an open flame or heat gun directly on a pipe it can cause the pipe to burst unpredictably or start a fire in the wall cavity.

Burst pipe (water is actively leaking):
The pipe has already split and water is flowing. Close the main water valve immediately (usually near the meter, in the garage or in a utility closet). Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker panel only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Photograph and video the damage before moving anything. Call (918) 395-9320 for extraction and drying. Don't wait for the plumber to finish the pipe repair before starting water removal every hour of delay expands the scope.

Where Does the Water Go After a Pipe Bursts in a Tulsa Home?

The visible water on the floor is only part of the story. Understanding how water travels through a home's structure explains why moisture mapping is essential and why the wet area is always larger than what you can see.

From a wall or ceiling pipe:
Water follows gravity and the path of least resistance inside the wall cavity. It runs down studs, along the bottom plate and across the subfloor. Drywall at the base of the wall begins wicking moisture upward by capillary action absorbing water 12 to 24 inches above the floor line within hours. If the break is in an interior wall, water can travel through the wall cavity to the other side and into the next room.

From an attic pipe:
This is the most destructive burst pipe scenario in Tulsa homes. Water drops from the attic onto the ceiling drywall, soaks through insulation, runs along joists in multiple directions and drips through light fixtures, can lights and seams potentially affecting multiple rooms below. By the time the homeowner notices water, the attic insulation is saturated, ceiling drywall is compromised and walls in several rooms may be wet.

From a crawlspace pipe:
Water pools on the crawlspace floor, saturates any insulation hanging beneath the subfloor and wicks upward into the subfloor and flooring above. In Tulsa's clay soil, crawlspace water doesn't drain it sits, raising humidity to levels that support mold growth on joists and subfloor within days.

From a slab pipe:
Water rises through the concrete slab and spreads laterally beneath flooring tile, hardwood, laminate or carpet across multiple rooms before surface signs appear. See

That's why we map moisture with thermal imaging and meters before we place a single piece of drying equipment. The map shows us where the water actually went not just where we can see it.

How Do We Handle Burst Pipe Water Damage?

Our burst-pipe response follows the same documented, IICRC S500-based process used for all water losses, with specific attention to the hidden water paths that pipe bursts create in Tulsa home construction.

Extraction
Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water from carpet, pad, hard floors and concrete. Carpet pad which absorbs water against the subfloor is almost always removed. Standing water in crawlspaces is pumped out with submersible pumps.

Moisture mapping
Thermal imaging and pin/pinless meters trace water inside every wall, ceiling and floor cavity the pipe could have reached. The moisture map drives equipment placement and daily monitoring. It also becomes a key document in your insurance claim.

Selective removal
Drywall that has wicked moisture significantly above the floor line is flood-cut the wet section is removed while dry drywall above is preserved. Saturated insulation in walls and attics is removed. Baseboards and trim along wet walls are pulled to allow air movement along the bottom of the wall. Every removal is photographed.

Structural drying
LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to the moisture map. Readings at every mapped point are taken daily and logged. Equipment is repositioned as areas reach their dry targets. The process typically takes 3 to 5 days for clean-water losses, longer for hardwood, plaster or heavy subfloor saturation. How Can I Prevent Frozen Pipes in My Tulsa Home?

Prevention doesn't eliminate the risk entirely, but it dramatically reduces the chance of a burst during a hard freeze. These steps are based on the failure patterns we see most in Tulsa homes.

  • Disconnect garden hoses from exterior spigots before the first freeze a connected hose traps water in the spigot and supply line, guaranteeing a freeze in cold weather
  • Insulate attic, garage and crawlspace lines standard thin-wall foam is adequate for mild freezes; for attic lines in northeastern Oklahoma, heavier insulation or heat tape is warranted
  • During a hard freeze (below 20°F): drip both hot and cold faucets on exterior walls, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air circulation and keep the garage door closed if supply lines run through the garage
  • Keep the thermostat at 55°F or above when traveling during winter never turn off heat entirely, even for short trips
  • Know where your main shut-off valve is before you need it finding it while water is pouring from the ceiling is not the time to search

Frequently Asked Questions

What do I do first when a pipe bursts?

+
Close the main water valve immediately usually near the meter, in the garage or in a utility closet. Then cut power to wet rooms at the breaker (only if you can reach the panel without standing in water), photograph the damage and call (918) 395-9320. Starting extraction quickly is the single most important factor in keeping the job and the cost small.

Is a burst pipe covered by insurance in Oklahoma?

+
Usually yes burst pipes are classified as a sudden and accidental loss under most Oklahoma homeowners policies, provided the home was being maintained and heated. The pipe repair itself (plumber's scope) may be covered separately or excluded depending on the policy. Gradual leaks and pipes that failed due to lack of maintenance are typically excluded. See:

How long does drying take after a burst pipe?

+
Most clean-water burst-pipe jobs take 3 to 5 days of active drying with commercial equipment. Hardwood floors, plaster walls and heavy subfloor saturation take longer sometimes 7 to 10 days. Equipment stays until daily moisture readings confirm every material has reached its dry target. Timeline guide:

Can the walls be saved after a burst pipe?

+
Often, yes if extraction starts quickly and the water was clean (Category 1). Drywall that wicked moisture only a few inches can often be dried in place with air movers directed along the base of the wall. Drywall that wicked significantly higher, sagged, delaminated or stayed wet beyond 24-48 hours is flood-cut and replaced. The decision is based on moisture-meter readings, not visual appearance.

Should I wait for the plumber before calling a restoration company?

+
No. Once you've shut off the main valve, the pipe repair and the water damage are separate scopes that can happen simultaneously. Waiting for the plumber to finish before starting extraction allows water to continue absorbing into drywall, subfloor and framing increasing the scope and cost significantly.
