What Counts as a Water Damage Emergency in Tulsa?

If water is spreading, sitting on any surface or contaminated, it qualifies as an emergency and the mitigation clock is already running. Waiting to see if it dries on its own is how a $2,000 extraction becomes a $10,000 tear-out-and-rebuild. Here are the emergency calls we handle most across Tulsa County.

Burst or frozen pipes
Attic, garage and exterior-wall supply lines split during January and February hard freezes when temperatures drop below 20°F. The pipe fails while frozen, but the leak starts when it thaws often while the homeowner is at work or asleep. Attic bursts are the most destructive because gravity carries water down through ceilings, walls and multiple rooms before it's noticed. Full details:

Flooded basement or crawlspace
Sump pump failure during a storm, hydrostatic pressure through foundation cracks, failed drain tile or burst pipes in the utility area. Basement water soaks framing and insulation from below and doesn't drain on its own in Tulsa's clay soil. See

Storm and roof breaches
Hail cracks shingles, straight-line wind lifts flashing and tornadoes remove sections of roof entirely. Rain enters the attic, soaks insulation and works down into ceiling drywall. Many homeowners don't know about the breach until they see a stain by then, insulation has been wet for days or weeks. See

Water heater and appliance failures
A standard 40- or 50-gallon water heater tank that corrodes and splits releases its full volume across the floor. Washing machine rubber supply hoses and refrigerator ice maker lines fail with similar effect. These almost always happen overnight. See

Sewage backup
Contaminated water coming up through floor drains, toilets or low fixtures. This is Category 3 grossly contaminated and porous materials it touches must be removed. Don't walk in it, don't try to clean it, and keep children and pets out. See

Slab leaks
Common in slab-on-grade homes across South Tulsa, Broken Arrow, Bixby and Owasso where Oklahoma's expansive clay soil stresses buried supply lines. A slab leak can run for days before someone notices a warm floor spot or an unexplained water bill increase by then, flooring and bottom plates across several rooms may be saturated. See

What Should I Do in the First 15 Minutes?

The actions you take between discovering water and the crew's arrival can save thousands of dollars in damage. These steps protect your home and strengthen your insurance claim.

  • Stop the water close the main shut-off valve (usually near the water meter, in the garage or in a utility closet). If you can't find it, turn off the valve at the street meter using a meter key. If the source is an appliance, close the appliance's individual shut-off valves.
  • Kill power to wet rooms switch off breakers for every room with water on the floor. Only do this if you can reach the breaker panel without walking through standing water. If the panel is in a wet area, call the electric company to disconnect power.
  • Get people and pets out clear any room with sagging ceilings, water near electrical outlets or the smell of sewage. A sagging ceiling can collapse without warning, and water near live outlets is an electrocution risk.
  • Photograph everything take wide-angle photos of each affected room, close-ups of the water source and video of the water level and spread. Do this before moving any furniture or belongings. These photos are your primary insurance evidence and your adjuster will use them to verify the loss.
  • Move what you can lift electronics, documents, photos and small valuables off wet floors. Place aluminum foil or plastic under furniture legs that can't be moved to prevent wood stain transfer to carpet.

What Happens When Our Crew Arrives?

Our arrival-to-drying process follows a documented sequence built to dry your home as fast as possible while creating the evidence your adjuster needs. Here's each step and why it matters.

Inspection and water classification
Before any equipment comes inside, we confirm the water source is stopped, check for electrical hazards (water near panels, outlets, appliances) and assess structural risks (sagging ceilings, buckled floors). Then we classify the water according to IICRC S500 standards the classification determines every decision that follows.

CategoryWhat it isHow it changes the job
Category 1 (clean)Supply line, water heater, ice makerMaterials can often be dried in place if extraction starts quickly
Category 2 (gray)Washer discharge, dishwasher, toilet overflow without fecesAntimicrobial treatment required; carpet pad and some porous materials removed
Category 3 (black)Sewage, floodwater from outside, any water sitting 72+ hoursAll porous materials removed and disposed; full structural disinfection

Full category and class guide:

Moisture mapping
Thermal imaging cameras detect temperature variations that indicate moisture behind drywall, above ceilings and under flooring areas that look dry from the surface. Pin and pinless moisture meters then take readings at each suspect point. The result is a documented moisture map showing exactly where water traveled, how high it wicked and how deeply it penetrated. This map serves three critical purposes: it tells us where to place equipment, it tracks drying progress day by day and it becomes a key document in your insurance claim.

Extraction
Truck-mounted extractors pull the bulk of standing water from carpet, pad, hard floors and concrete. They remove far more water, far faster, than any portable or consumer-grade equipment. Portable extractors reach tight areas closets, under cabinets, utility rooms. After extraction, we assess every wet material. Saturated carpet pad is almost always removed because it holds water against the subfloor and breeds bacteria. Drywall that has wicked water more than a few inches above the floor line is typically cut out because drying it in place takes too long and risks mold growth inside the wall cavity.

Structural drying
LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to the moisture map and sized to the room volume, temperature and moisture load. LGR dehumidifiers are specifically effective in Tulsa's humid climate because they pull moisture at lower grain-depression levels than standard refrigerant units critical during summer months when ambient humidity exceeds 70%.

Readings are taken daily at every mapped point. Equipment is repositioned as drying progresses wet spots that weren't exposed yesterday become the focus today. Drying continues until every material reaches its documented dry target, set against unaffected reference materials in the same home per IICRC S500 protocol. Full details on the drying process:

Sanitizing and mold prevention
Every affected surface receives EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment. This step is essential because mold spores are always present in indoor air they only need moisture and food (drywall paper, wood) to colonize. By treating surfaces before mold has a chance to establish, we close the window. If mold growth is already visible when we arrive, our team handles it within the same project under IICRC S520 containment protocols.

Documentation and repairs
The complete documentation package is assembled throughout the project and delivered to you and your adjuster:

  • Pre-mitigation photos and video
  • Moisture maps with readings at each point
  • Daily drying logs showing the drying curve
  • Equipment placement and runtime records
  • Material removal documentation
  • Antimicrobial treatment records
  • Final dry verification readings
  • Written mitigation scope
  • Separate written restoration scope for drywall, flooring and finish repairs

Why Does Tulsa Water Damage Turn Into Mold So Fast?

Tulsa's climate creates conditions where mold grows faster than in most U.S. cities, and understanding why helps explain the urgency behind emergency response timelines.

Humidity. Tulsa summers regularly exceed 70% relative humidity, with July and August dew points above 65°F. Indoor humidity in a water-damaged home can spike above 80% within hours of a loss well above the 60% threshold where mold growth accelerates.

Clay soil. Oklahoma's expansive clay soil holds moisture against foundations, crawlspaces and slab edges long after the interior surface looks dry. Pier-and-beam homes in Midtown, Brookside and North Tulsa sit over crawlspaces where ground moisture attacks floor joists from below creating conditions where mold can grow for months without anyone seeing it.

Drywall paper. Modern drywall is faced with paper one of the best food sources for mold. Once the paper is wet, spores that are always present in indoor air begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours. Behind a wall, where airflow is minimal and humidity stays high, mold can spread across an entire wall cavity in a week.

Delayed response. Many Tulsa homeowners attempt to dry water damage with box fans and household dehumidifiers, which dry the surface but leave moisture trapped inside wall cavities and subfloor. The surface looks dry within a day or two, but the readings inside the wall haven't changed and mold begins growing on the hidden side of the drywall.
