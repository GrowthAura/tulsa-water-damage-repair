Why Is Sewage Backup Dangerous?

Sewage backup is the most hazardous water damage category a Tulsa homeowner can face, and understanding the contamination level explains why the cleanup protocols are significantly more aggressive than those used for clean water.

Under IICRC S500 water categories, sewage is classified as Category 3 grossly contaminated. This classification means the water contains pathogenic organisms capable of causing serious illness or death. Sewage can carry E. coli, norovirus, hepatitis A, Salmonella, Giardia and other bacteria and viruses that cause illness through direct skin contact, ingestion or inhalation of contaminated aerosol droplets.

Why household cleaning isn't adequate:
Household bleach kills some surface bacteria, but it cannot penetrate porous materials carpet fibers, carpet pad, drywall paper, wood grain, insulation. Once sewage is absorbed into these materials, the contamination is inside the material where no surface disinfectant can reach. The material must be physically removed, bagged and disposed of.

Why fans and dehumidifiers alone make it worse:
Air movement across sewage-contaminated surfaces without proper containment aerosolizes bacteria spreading contamination through the HVAC system to clean rooms throughout the home.

What Causes Sewage Backups in Tulsa?

Sewage backups in the Tulsa metro have specific, identifiable causes related to the city's sewer infrastructure, soil conditions, tree population and weather patterns.

Overloaded city sewer lines during heavy rain
Tulsa's sewer system particularly in older neighborhoods like Midtown, North Tulsa and West Tulsa can be overwhelmed by heavy rainfall that enters the system through inflow and infiltration. When the system's capacity is exceeded, sewage backs up through the lowest discharge points in connected homes: floor drains, basement toilets and ground-level fixtures.

Tree roots in sewer laterals
The private sewer lateral connecting your home to the city main runs through your yard through soil filled with tree roots. Mature oaks, elms and other established trees in Tulsa's older neighborhoods send roots directly into cast-iron and clay sewer pipes through joints and cracks. Over years, root intrusion narrows the pipe until a partial blockage becomes a full backup.

Grease, wipes and debris
Cooking grease that solidifies in the sewer line, "flushable" wipes that don't break down and other debris create blockages in the private lateral. These backups typically affect only your home not neighbors because the blockage is between your house and the city main.

Collapsed lateral lines
Oklahoma's expansive clay soil puts seasonal stress on older sewer laterals particularly cast-iron and orangeburg (bituminized fiber) pipes. Sections can collapse or separate, creating complete blockages.

Septic system failures
Rural Rogers, Osage and Creek County homes on septic systems face tank overflow, drain field failure and pump malfunction that push sewage back into the home through fixtures.

Flood events
During major flooding like the 2019 Arkansas River flood that hit Sand Springs , Jenks and Bixby floodwater enters the sewer system from the river side, overwhelming capacity and pushing sewage up through every connected low point. See

How Do We Clean Up Sewage Safely?

Sewage cleanup follows a strict containment-first protocol designed to protect the crew, prevent cross-contamination to clean areas and decontaminate the affected space to a level safe for reoccupancy.

1. Containment
Before extraction begins, poly sheeting and negative air machines seal off the contaminated area from the rest of the home. Negative air pressure ensures that air flows into the work area, not out preventing contaminated aerosols from reaching clean rooms. All crew members wear full protective equipment: Tyvek suits, chemical-resistant gloves, rubber boots and N95 or P100 respirators.

2. Extraction
All standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted and portable equipment. Extraction equipment used in Category 3 losses is decontaminated before it leaves the work area and before it's used on any other job.

3. Contaminated material removal
Every porous material that contacted sewage is removed, bagged and disposed of. This includes carpet, carpet pad, drywall (cut 12-24 inches above the sewage line to account for wicking), insulation, baseboards and any upholstered or fabric items that were in contact. All materials are photographed before removal for your insurance claim.

4. Clean, then disinfect
This is a two-step process not just spraying disinfectant on dirty surfaces. Exposed framing, subfloor, concrete and remaining non-porous surfaces are first cleaned with a detergent solution to remove organic matter and biofilm. Then EPA-registered disinfectants formulated specifically for Category 3 contamination are applied. This two-step approach is critical because disinfectants cannot penetrate biofilm the organic layer must be removed first for the disinfectant to reach the pathogens.

5. HEPA filtration and odor control
HEPA air scrubbers run throughout the cleanup to capture airborne contaminants. After disinfection, odor treatment addresses the residual sewage smell that persists in structural materials even after cleaning.

6. Structural drying and rebuild
The structure is dried to measured targets using LGR dehumidifiers and air movers with daily moisture monitoring. Drying follows IICRC S500 standards the same protocol used for all water losses, applied after the contamination is removed. Full drying details: . Once dry, drywall, insulation, flooring and finishes are rebuilt.

What Should I Do Right Now If Sewage Is in My Home?

Time and safety are the two priorities. Every minute of exposure increases contamination spread, and direct contact with sewage is a health risk.

  • Stop using all plumbing sinks, toilets, showers, washing machine, dishwasher. Any additional water flow into the sewer line can push more sewage into the home.
  • Keep everyone out of the affected area children, pets and anyone with respiratory or immune conditions should leave the home entirely if possible.
  • Don't touch the water without rubber boots and chemical-resistant gloves sewage pathogens enter through skin contact, especially through cuts or abrasions.
  • Turn off the HVAC system if it serves the affected space the system can circulate contaminated air and spread sewage aerosols to clean areas.
  • Don't mop, sweep or try to clean it disturbing sewage without containment spreads it.
  • Photograph from a safe distance document the affected area for your insurance claim without walking through the contamination.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backup in Oklahoma?

+
Usually only if you have a sewer and drain backup endorsement on your policy. Without it, standard Oklahoma homeowners policies typically exclude sewer backup. This endorsement is relatively inexpensive usually from $40 to $100 per year and covers one of the most expensive types of water damage. Check your declarations page or ask your agent. Full guide:

Can sewage-soaked carpet be cleaned?

+
No. Carpet and carpet pad that absorbed Category 3 water are removed and disposed of no amount of cleaning can decontaminate a porous material that has absorbed sewage throughout its thickness. Hard, non-porous surfaces (tile, sealed concrete, vinyl) can be cleaned and disinfected.

Is it safe to stay home during sewage cleanup?

+
We recommend staying out of the affected area entirely. For children, elderly family members and anyone with respiratory conditions or immune concerns, staying elsewhere until disinfection is complete and air filtration has cleared the space is strongly recommended.

Will sewage cause mold?

+
It can sewage brings moisture and mold spores together. However, because porous materials are removed quickly during Category 3 cleanup, the mold risk is managed by the removal process itself. If growth is found on framing or subfloor during material removal, it's handled through our process within the same project.

What's the difference between a sewer backup and a septic backup?

+
A sewer backup comes through the municipal sewer system caused by overloaded mains, tree roots, blockages or flooding. A septic backup comes from a failed on-site septic system tank overflow, drain field failure or pump malfunction. Both produce Category 3 contaminated water and require the same cleanup protocols.
