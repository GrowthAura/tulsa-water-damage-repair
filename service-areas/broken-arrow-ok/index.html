What Causes Most Water Damage in Broken Arrow?

Broken Arrow's water damage patterns are driven by two factors: the age distribution of its housing stock and its position in the northeastern Oklahoma hail corridor.

Slab leaks in 1980s-2000s homes
These homes now 25 to 40+ years old are entering the age window where buried copper and CPVC supply lines fail under Oklahoma's reactive clay soil. Seasonal soil movement stresses joints and pipe walls over decades until pinhole leaks or joint separations occur. A slab leak can wet flooring and wall bottoms across multiple rooms for weeks before the homeowner notices a warm floor spot, cracked tile or a water bill increase. See

Hail and spring storms
One severe storm can damage hundreds of Broken Arrow roofs in a single evening. The northeast Oklahoma hail corridor runs directly through Broken Arrow, and the city's exposure extends from April through June with occasional fall events. Ceiling stains from hail-damaged roofs often appear weeks after the storm, once insulation has absorbed enough water from subsequent rains to soak through the drywall below. See and

Winter pipe freezes
Exterior-wall and attic supply lines in eastern Broken Arrow subdivisions where construction often routes water lines through attics and exterior walls with light insulation split during January and February hard freezes. The leak starts when the pipe thaws, sometimes while the homeowner is at work. See

Water heaters and AC overflows
Aging water heater tanks in 15-to-25-year-old homes fail overnight, releasing their full volume across utility closets and adjacent rooms. Attic-mounted AC air handlers with clogged condensate lines overflow onto ceiling drywall during peak summer. See and

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my Broken Arrow home has a slab leak?

+
Watch for these signs: warm or damp spots on the floor, the sound of running water when all fixtures are off, cracked tile or shifting grout lines, soft drywall at wall bases and a water bill that increases without explanation. A plumber can locate and confirm the leak; call us at (918) 395-9320 for the resulting water damage to flooring, drywall and structure. See

Is slab leak damage covered by insurance?

+
The water damage resulting from a slab leak wet flooring, drywall, insulation is usually covered under standard Oklahoma homeowners policies. Breaking the slab to access and repair the pipe is often excluded or covered separately. The distinction varies by policy. See:

What's the most common water damage call in Broken Arrow?

+
In winter, it's pipe bursts in attics and exterior walls. In spring, it's hail-related roof leaks. In summer, it's AC condensate overflows. Year-round, slab leaks and water heater failures are consistent calls particularly in homes 20 to 30 years old.

How do I prevent frozen pipes in my Broken Arrow home?

+
