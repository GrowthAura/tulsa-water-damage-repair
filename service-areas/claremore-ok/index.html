What Causes Water Damage in Claremore Homes?

Claremore's housing stock spans more than a century, and each era brings its own vulnerabilities. The causes we see most in Rogers County are shaped by the age of the home, its foundation type and its location.

Older homes near downtown and the Will Rogers Memorial
Homes built between the 1910s and 1960s around downtown Claremore commonly have galvanized steel supply lines, cast-iron drain lines, plaster walls and pier-and-beam foundations over crawlspaces. Galvanized pipes corrode from the inside over decades, developing pinhole leaks that drip inside walls for weeks before anyone notices. Cast-iron drains eventually crack and leak under slabs and through crawlspaces. Plaster walls hold water far longer than modern drywall, and the crawlspaces beneath these homes trap moisture against floor joists and subfloor creating conditions where mold grows quietly for months or years.

Hard winter freezes
Rogers County cold snaps January and February nights that drop below 15°F split water lines in attics, garages and exterior walls. The pipe fails while frozen, but the leak doesn't start until it thaws, often hours later when the homeowner is at work or still asleep. Attic pipe bursts are especially destructive because water travels down through multiple ceilings and walls before it's noticed. See

Spring hail and tornado season
Oklahoma's March-through-June storm season brings hail and wind that crack shingles, lift flashing and break roof seals. Rain enters the attic and soaks insulation but the insulation acts as a sponge, absorbing water silently for days or weeks before enough accumulates to stain the ceiling below. By the time the homeowner sees the stain, the insulation has been wet long enough for mold to establish. See

Verdigris River and local creeks
Low-lying properties near the Verdigris River and smaller Claremore-area creeks are exposed to flooding during prolonged rain events. Floodwater from outside is always Category 3 grossly contaminated regardless of how clear it appears. Porous materials it contacts must be removed, not dried. Rising water is not covered by standard homeowners insurance; it requires a separate flood policy. See

Rural wells and septic systems
Outlying Rogers County properties on well water and septic systems face failure modes that municipal-connected homes don't. Well pump failures cause loss of water pressure and can damage pressure tanks. Septic system backups push Category 3 contaminated water into the home through floor drains and low fixtures. Septic backups require the same protocols as sewage backups: full removal of porous materials and structural disinfection. See

What Should I Do If Water Damage Happens in My Claremore Home?

The same first-response steps apply in Claremore as anywhere in our service area and taking them quickly is the most important thing you can do to reduce the scope of damage.

  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe, fixture or appliance
  • Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water
  • Move electronics, documents and valuables off wet floors
  • Photograph and video everything before moving anything this is your insurance evidence

Is mold common in older Claremore homes?

+
Very. Crawlspaces and plaster walls in homes built before the 1960s hold moisture for extended periods, and without a vapor barrier under pier-and-beam foundations, ground moisture feeds mold growth on joists and subfloor year-round. Any musty smell or visible spots should be assessed for remediation. See

Will insurance cover my Claremore water damage?

+
Sudden and accidental losses burst pipes, failed water heaters, broken supply lines are usually covered under standard Oklahoma homeowners policies.
