What Water Damage Risks Are Specific to Jenks?

Jenks faces water damage risks that other Tulsa-area communities don't share primarily because of its position on the Arkansas River and the age of its rapid-growth housing stock. Understanding these risks helps Jenks homeowners prepare for the most likely scenarios.

Arkansas River flooding
During the May-June 2019 flood, Keystone Dam releases pushed the Arkansas River to levels not recorded since 1986. Low-lying Jenks properties near the river corridor took on water including neighborhoods that had never flooded in their history. Rising river water is always classified as Category 3 (grossly contaminated) under IICRC S500 standards, which means every porous material it touches must be removed, not dried.

Standard Oklahoma homeowners insurance policies do not cover rising water from outside the home. River flooding requires a separate National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policy, which carries a 30-day waiting period from purchase before coverage activates. If you're within or near the mapped floodplain, NFIP coverage should be in place before flood season. See

Sewer backup during floods
When Arkansas River flooding or heavy rain overloads the sewer system, contaminated water can be forced up through floor drains, low toilets and other fixtures. Sewer backup is also Category 3 grossly contaminated and requires full removal of porous materials, structural disinfection and protective equipment during cleanup. Most standard homeowners policies exclude sewer backup unless a specific endorsement has been added. See

Slab leaks in newer subdivisions
Much of Jenks was built between the 1990s and 2010s on slab-on-grade foundations over Oklahoma's expansive clay soil. The copper supply lines buried within and beneath these slabs are now old enough to fail as the soil's seasonal movement swelling after rain, shrinking during drought weakens joints and creates pinhole leaks. Signs include warm or damp floor spots, the sound of running water when nothing is on, cracked tile and unexplained increases in the water bill. See

Hail and wind damage
Jenks sits squarely in Oklahoma's spring storm track. Hail cracks shingles across whole neighborhoods in a single event, and many homeowners don't file a roof claim until rain has already entered the attic through the damaged area. Water soaks insulation and sits on ceiling drywall for days or weeks before a stain appears by then, mold has started in the insulation. See

Insurance coverage for water damage in Jenks depends entirely on where the water came from, and the distinction between "water damage" and "flood damage" in insurance terms is different from how most homeowners use those words.

What Should Jenks Homeowners Do Before Flood Season?

Preparation doesn't prevent flooding, but it reduces damage, speeds recovery and protects your insurance position.

  • Check your flood zone search your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center. Properties in the Special Flood Hazard Area should carry NFIP coverage.
  • Buy flood insurance early NFIP policies have a 30-day waiting period. Buying during a flood watch is too late.
  • Add sewer backup coverage ask your insurance agent about adding a sewer backup endorsement. It's typically inexpensive relative to the cost of a Category 3 cleanup.
  • Know your shut-off valve location you'll need to find it fast if water enters the home.
  • Keep a documentation kit a charged phone, a list of your agent's contact information and your policy number in an accessible place.
