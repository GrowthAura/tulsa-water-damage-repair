How Do We Work?

The difference between a good water damage job and a bad one isn't equipment or manpower it's whether someone is measuring, documenting and making decisions based on what the readings say instead of what the surface looks like. Here's how we approach every job.

We measure, we don't guess
Every job starts with moisture mapping thermal imaging cameras and pin/pinless moisture meters that show where water actually traveled, not just where it's visible. Every drying day ends with logged readings at every mapped point. Equipment comes out when the numbers confirm dry, not when a calendar says it's been long enough. This approach catches the hidden moisture that box-fan drying misses the moisture that becomes mold inside wall cavities weeks later.

We follow industry standards
Water drying follows the IICRC S500 standard the same reference document insurance adjusters use to evaluate mitigation claims. Mold work follows IICRC S520 guidelines. Following these standards isn't a marketing claim it means the work is documented in the language and format adjusters already know, which makes claims easier to review and harder to dispute. See

We document everything
Moisture maps, daily drying logs, equipment placement and runtime records, photos before, during and after, and a written itemized scope for every job, not just large or insurance-funded ones. This documentation serves two purposes: it's how we verify the work is complete, and it's how your adjuster verifies the claim is legitimate. See

We explain before we cut
You'll know what's being removed and why before anything comes out of your walls, ceilings or floors. Every removal decision is based on moisture readings, water category and material condition not a blanket policy of removing everything. When plaster or hardwood can be saved with longer drying, we save it. When material has to come out, you'll understand the reading or condition that made that decision before the first cut happens.

Why Does Knowing Tulsa Matter for Water Damage Restoration?

Water damage isn't generic it behaves differently based on the building materials, foundation type, soil conditions, climate and geography of the specific home in the specific city. Tulsa's conditions create patterns that a company unfamiliar with them will miss.

Building materials vary by neighborhood. A 1925 Maple Ridge home with plaster walls, original oak floors and a pier-and-beam crawlspace is a fundamentally different drying job than a 2005 Broken Arrow slab home with drywall and carpet. Plaster takes longer. Hardwood needs mat drying. The crawlspace must be dried simultaneously. Applying a one-size-fits-all drywall-timeline approach to plaster leaves moisture in the wall. See

Clay soil affects every foundation type. Oklahoma's expansive clay swells after rain and shrinks during drought. In slab homes, this movement stresses buried supply lines. In pier-and-beam homes, it keeps crawlspaces damp year-round. In basements, it creates hydrostatic pressure that pushes water through walls. The soil doesn't just cause damage it extends drying times and sustains mold conditions.

Summer humidity accelerates mold. July and August dew points above 65°F and relative humidity above 70% mean that wet drywall, wood and insulation in a Tulsa home can start growing mold in as little as 24 hours during summer faster than the national 24-to-48-hour average. See

Hail season is predictable. April through June brings hail that damages thousands of roofs across the metro every year. The resulting water damage follows a predictable pattern insulation absorbs rain, ceiling stains appear weeks later, mold starts in the attic. Crews that know this pattern check attic insulation on every ceiling-stain call. See

The Arkansas River floods. The 2019 flood hit Sand Springs , West Tulsa, Jenks and Bixby . Homes that flooded and weren't fully dried still show mold years later. River-corridor properties need flood insurance, not just homeowners insurance. See

We plan around these conditions on every job because they're the conditions every Tulsa job actually faces.
