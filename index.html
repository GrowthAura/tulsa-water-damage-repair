What Does Water Damage Restoration in Tulsa Include?

Water damage restoration is not a single step it runs in two distinct phases that serve different purposes, and understanding both helps you know what to expect from the process, the timeline and the cost.

Phase 1 Mitigation stops the damage from spreading. We locate and stop the water source, extract all standing water with truck-mounted and portable equipment, remove materials that can't be saved (saturated carpet pad, wicking drywall, soaked insulation), and dry the structure until moisture meters confirm each material has reached its documented dry target. Every job follows IICRC S500 drying standards readings are logged daily, and equipment is pulled when the numbers say dry, not on a calendar schedule.

Phase 2 Restoration rebuilds what mitigation removed. This includes hanging new drywall, replacing insulation, installing flooring, fitting trim and repainting to match existing finishes. The restoration scope is written separately so your insurance adjuster can review each phase on its own.

The distinction matters because mitigation is time-critical every hour of delay increases the scope while restoration can be scheduled once the structure is verified dry. Read how the drying phase works in detail on our page.

What Causes Most Water Damage in Tulsa Homes?

Tulsa's climate, soil and housing stock combine to create water damage risks that repeat year after year. Understanding these causes helps homeowners take preventive steps and recognize the signs before a small issue becomes a major loss.

Winter pipe freezes
Attic, garage and exterior-wall pipes are the most common failure point during January and February cold snaps. When temperatures drop below freezing, water inside uninsulated or under-insulated pipes expands and splits the fitting or the pipe itself. The leak doesn't start until the pipe thaws sometimes hours later which is why homeowners wake up to water pouring through a ceiling. Thousands of Tulsa-area pipes failed during the February 2021 arctic blast, overwhelming restoration companies across Green Country for weeks. Full details on how we handle this:

Spring hail and tornado storms
Oklahoma's spring storm season March through June brings hail, straight-line wind and tornadoes. Even moderate hail can crack shingles and break the seal on flashing. Rain doesn't enter the home immediately; it soaks into attic insulation over days or weeks, and homeowners only notice when a ceiling stain appears or insulation sags through a can light. By that point, the wet insulation has been sitting on the drywall long enough for mold to start. See

Slab leaks
Oklahoma's expansive clay soil prevalent across South Tulsa, Broken Arrow, Bixby and Owasso swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That seasonal movement puts stress on the copper supply lines buried under slab-on-grade foundations, causing pinhole leaks or joint failures. Clues include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, cracked tile and a water bill that jumps without explanation. A plumber locates and repairs the leak; we handle the water damage to flooring and structure. See

Attic AC condensate overflows
Central air conditioners produce condensation that drains through a PVC line usually routed through the attic in Tulsa homes. When that line clogs with algae or debris (most commonly in July and August when the system runs constantly), the condensate pan overflows and water pours down into the ceiling, walls and insulation below. A single clogged line can soak a bedroom ceiling in a day. See

Water heater and appliance failures
A standard tank water heater has an expected lifespan of 8 to 12 years. Past that window, the tank can corrode internally and fail often overnight, releasing 40 to 50 gallons across a utility closet, hallway and adjacent rooms. Rubber washing machine supply hoses and refrigerator ice maker lines are the other common overnight failures. See

River and creek flooding
The 2019 Arkansas River flood hit Sand Springs, West Tulsa, Jenks and Bixby when Keystone Dam releases pushed the river to levels not recorded since 1986. Flash flooding along Mingo Creek, Joe Creek and Bird Creek can follow any stalled spring storm. Floodwater from outside is classified as Category 3 grossly contaminated and porous materials it contacts must be removed. See

Why Do the First 24-48 Hours Matter So Much?

The timeline of water damage is measured in hours, not days, and each stage makes the job larger, more complex and more expensive. Understanding this progression explains why restoration professionals treat water damage as an emergency even when the visible damage looks minor.

Within minutes: water spreads across hard floors and begins wicking into carpet pad. Pad absorbs water rapidly and holds it against the subfloor.

Within 1-4 hours: drywall begins absorbing moisture by capillary action, wicking water upward from the floor line. Wood trim swells. Carpet pad is fully saturated in the affected area.

Within 4-24 hours: drywall has wicked moisture 12 to 24 inches above the floor line (higher in some cases). Subfloor particularly OSB begins to swell. Humidity in the room climbs. Clean supply-line water that has been sitting begins to degrade as it picks up contaminants from surfaces, carpet and soil.

Within 24-48 hours: mold can begin colonizing wet drywall paper and wood framing the window the EPA cites for active drying. What started as Category 1 clean water may have degraded into Category 2 gray water, which requires antimicrobial treatment and makes more materials unsalvageable. The scope has now expanded from a drying job into a removal-and-drying job.

Beyond 48 hours: mold spreads behind walls where it isn't visible. Drywall paper delaminates. Subfloor warps. Structural framing begins absorbing moisture. The cost and timeline increase significantly.

Starting extraction early is the single biggest factor in keeping the scope and the bill small. That's why we respond around the clock with equipment already on the truck. See

How Does Our Restoration Process Work?

Our process follows a documented, repeatable sequence designed to dry Tulsa homes as fast as possible while building the evidence your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim. Each step builds on the one before it.

Step 1 Call and dispatch
A dispatcher takes your address, the water source and the approximate area affected. The nearest available crew heads out with extraction and drying equipment already loaded no stop at a warehouse. You get a realistic arrival window before we hang up.

Step 2 Inspection and water classification
On arrival, we confirm the water source is stopped (or stop it), check for electrical and structural hazards, and classify the water according to IICRC S500 standards. Classification determines how the job is handled:

CategorySource exampleWhat it means
Category 1 (clean)Supply line, water heaterMany materials can be dried in place if extraction is fast
Category 2 (gray)Washer discharge, toilet overflow without wasteAntimicrobial treatment required; carpet pad and some porous materials removed
Category 3 (black)Sewage, floodwater from outsideAll porous materials removed; full disinfection of structure

Full guide to categories and classes:

Step 3 Moisture mapping
Thermal imaging cameras show temperature differences that indicate moisture behind walls, above ceilings and under floors. Pin and pinless moisture meters then confirm readings at each point. The resulting moisture map drives the drying plan it tells us exactly where to place equipment and becomes a key document in your claim file.

Step 4 Extraction
Truck-mounted extractors remove the bulk of standing water from carpet, pad and hard floors. Portable units reach tight areas. Materials that are saturated beyond recovery wicking drywall, soaked carpet pad, collapsed insulation are removed so the structure behind them can dry. Every removed item is documented with photos.

Step 5 Structural drying
LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to the moisture map and sized to the room volume and moisture load. Readings are taken daily at each mapped point. Equipment is repositioned as needed and stays until every material reaches its dry target not a fixed number of days. Full details on our page.

Step 6 Sanitizing and documentation
Affected surfaces receive EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to prevent mold colonization. You receive a complete documentation package: moisture maps, daily drying logs, equipment placement records and photos. This package is formatted for your adjuster and supports your claim from day one. See our insurance claim guide:

What Should I Do Before the Crew Arrives?

The steps you take in the first 15 minutes after discovering water damage can significantly affect how much of your home can be saved. Here's what to do and what to avoid while waiting for the crew.

Do these things immediately:

  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe, fixture or appliance the valve is usually near the meter, in the garage or in a utility closet
  • Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker panel only if you can reach the panel without standing in water
  • Move electronics, area rugs, documents and valuables out of the wet area and onto dry surfaces or into dry rooms
  • Photograph and video everything before moving anything shoot wide angles of each room plus close-ups of where the water is coming from; this is your primary insurance evidence
  • Place aluminum foil or plastic under furniture legs to prevent staining on wet carpet

Avoid these common mistakes:

  • Don't use a household vacuum on standing water it's not designed for water and creates an electrical hazard
  • Don't run the HVAC system if water may have reached ductwork the system can spread moisture and contaminants to dry areas
  • Don't pull up carpet yourself it may be salvageable, and removing it incorrectly can damage the subfloor
  • Don't spray bleach or cleaning products on wet drywall it doesn't stop mold and it can complicate professional treatment

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Oklahoma?

+
Most standard Oklahoma homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage a burst pipe, failed water heater or broken supply line. Rising floodwater from outside the home needs a separate National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policy. Sewer backup usually needs an endorsement added to your policy. Long-term gradual leaks are commonly excluded. The key to a smooth claim is documentation: moisture maps, daily drying logs and an itemized scope. Full details:

How long does it take to dry a water-damaged house?

+
Most clean-water jobs dry in 3 to 5 days with commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers. Hardwood floors, plaster walls, concrete slabs and contaminated water extend the timeline. Equipment stays until moisture readings taken daily at every mapped point confirm each material has reached its dry target. See the full drying timeline guide:

Can I dry water damage myself with fans?

+
Box fans and household dehumidifiers dry surface moisture, but they cannot reach water trapped inside wall cavities, beneath subfloor or within insulation. That hidden moisture is exactly what causes mold growth and structural odor weeks or months later. Anything beyond a small spill caught immediately on a hard floor needs commercial extraction, dehumidification and daily moisture readings to verify the structure is genuinely dry.

What is the difference between clean, gray and black water?

+
Category 1 (clean) comes from a sanitary source like a supply line or water heater. Category 2 (gray) carries chemical or biological contaminants washer discharge, dishwasher overflow, toilet overflow with urine but no feces. Category 3 (black) is grossly contaminated sewage, floodwater from outside, or any water that has been sitting long enough to degrade.
