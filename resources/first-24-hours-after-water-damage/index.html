What to Do in the First 24 Hours After Water Damage

The first 24 hours after water damage decide two things: whether you're drying a room or rebuilding one, and whether your insurance claim pays fully or gets reduced. Mold can start growing on wet drywall and wood in 24 to 48 hours faster in Tulsa's summer humidity and clean water that sits for more than 48 hours can degrade into contaminated water that requires material removal instead of drying. Everything you do in the first day is about speed, safety and evidence.

Minute 0 Stop the Water and Stay Safe

The first minutes determine whether water continues spreading into additional rooms and materials. These steps address safety first, then stop the water source.

Stop the water:

  • Close the main water valve if the source is a pipe, fixture or appliance. The valve is usually near the water meter (often in the front yard or garage), in a utility closet or in the basement. If you can't find it, close the valve at the street meter using a meter key.
  • If the source is a specific appliance (washer, water heater, dishwasher), close the individual shut-off valves behind or near the appliance.
  • If the source is a roof breach or storm, cover the opening from inside with plastic sheeting if safe never climb on a damaged roof during or after a storm.

Stay safe:

  • Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker panel only if you can reach the panel without walking through standing water. If the panel is in a wet area, call your electric company to disconnect power at the meter.
  • Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings water-loaded drywall and saturated insulation can collapse without warning.
  • Don't walk through water near electrical outlets even if you think the power is off, wet wiring can conduct current.
  • Smell gas? Leave the house immediately and call the gas company from outside. Don't flip switches or use phones near the smell.
  • Sewage involved? Keep everyone including pets completely out of the affected area. Don't touch sewage water without rubber boots and chemical-resistant gloves. Don't try to clean it with household products. See

Document everything before anything moves. Your adjuster wasn't there when the water was on the floor your photos and video are their primary evidence of the initial loss.

  • Shoot a slow video walk-through of every wet room, capturing floors, walls, ceilings and the water source
  • Take close-up photos of the point of failure the burst pipe joint, the failed tank, the appliance, the roof breach
  • Photograph standing water with something showing depth a ruler, a tape measure, a shoe
  • Capture serial numbers and model numbers of damaged appliances
  • Don't move furniture or belongings until you've photographed their position in the water

Call your insurance carrier and open the claim. Report the loss the same day delayed reporting is one of the most common reasons claims get reduced. Write down the claim number, the representative's name and when the adjuster will contact you. Full claim guide:

Hours 1-4 Protect Your Belongings

While waiting for the crew or during the initial stages of extraction, protect what you can. Every item you save from water contact is one fewer item on the claim and one fewer thing to replace.

  • Move electronics (computers, TVs, gaming consoles, chargers) off wet floors and onto dry surfaces in dry rooms electronics and standing water are both an electrical hazard and a total-loss risk
  • Move documents, photos, books and important papers to dry areas wet paper is extremely difficult to restore
  • Roll up area rugs that are on wet floors and move them to a dry space area rugs often trap water beneath them, accelerating subfloor damage
  • Place aluminum foil or plastic under furniture legs that can't be moved off wet carpet this prevents the wood stain from furniture legs from permanently discoloring carpet
  • Hang up wet clothing and fabrics on a line or over a shower rod if they're from clean water don't put wet items in a pile
  • Turn off the HVAC system if water reached ductwork, the air handler or return vents the system can spread moisture and contaminants to dry areas throughout the house
  • If water entered from a storm breach, cover the opening from inside with plastic sheeting to slow additional rain entry exterior tarping by a crew is the permanent solution. See

Hours 4-24 Professional Extraction and Drying Begins

By the 4-hour mark, professional extraction should be underway. Here's what happens during this critical window and what you should see from the crew.

Water classification
The crew classifies the water according to IICRC S500 standards this determines what can be dried in place and what must be removed. Category 1 (clean supply-line water) offers the most drying options. Category 2 (gray water from washers, dishwashers) requires antimicrobial treatment. Category 3 (sewage, floodwater) requires removal of all porous materials. See

Moisture mapping
Thermal imaging cameras and pin/pinless moisture meters trace water through walls, above ceilings and beneath floors. The resulting map shows the full extent of moisture almost always larger than the visible wet area and drives every equipment placement decision.

Extraction
Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water from carpet, pad, hard floors and concrete. Carpet pad which absorbs water and holds it against the subfloor is almost always removed.

Selective demolition
Drywall that has wicked moisture significantly above the floor line is flood-cut. Saturated insulation is removed. Baseboards are pulled to allow air movement at the base of walls.

Drying equipment placement
LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to the moisture map and sized to the room volume and moisture load.

Antimicrobial treatment
EPA-registered antimicrobials are applied to affected surfaces to prevent mold colonization during the drying window.

Initial readings logged
Moisture readings at every mapped point are documented this is the baseline that drying progress is measured against daily. See

Why Does Speed Matter So Much in Tulsa Specifically?

The 24-to-48-hour mold growth window cited nationally by the EPA and FEMA is an average. In the Tulsa metro, several factors can shorten this window making the first 24 hours even more critical here than in less humid climates.

Summer humidity July and August dew points above 65°F and relative humidity above 70% mean indoor humidity in a water-damaged home can exceed 80% within hours. Above 80%, mold growth can start in as little as 12 to 18 hours on ideal surfaces like drywall paper and carpet pad.

Warm wall cavities Tulsa homes heat up during summer, and wall cavities reach temperatures in the optimal mold growth range (77-86°F). A wall cavity that's warm, humid and dark is a perfect incubator.

Clay soil keeps crawlspaces, slab edges and basements damp for days after the surface looks dry, sustaining elevated moisture even after room-level extraction.

Hidden water paths Tulsa's most common hidden water sources (attic AC overflows, slab leaks, slow supply drips) wet materials in concealed spaces where damage progresses undetected.

Fast extraction keeps clean-water damage in the "dry it" category instead of the "tear it out and disinfect it" category. See

After the First 24 Hours What Comes Next?

Once extraction is complete and drying equipment is running, the job enters the monitoring phase.

Days 1-5: daily moisture readings at every mapped point track the drying curve. Equipment is repositioned as areas reach their dry targets. The crew visits daily to read meters, adjust equipment and log progress.

Day 3-5 (typical): clean-water losses in drywall and carpet reach dry targets. Hardwood, plaster, concrete and contaminated-water losses take longer. See

When readings confirm dry: equipment is removed. Antimicrobial treatment is verified. Documentation is finalized moisture maps, daily logs, photos and a written scope and delivered to you and your adjuster. See

Rebuild: drywall, insulation, flooring, trim and paint are replaced. The restoration scope is written separately from the mitigation scope so your adjuster can review each phase independently.

Frequently Asked Questions

I found the water the next morning is it too late?

+
No. Overnight water means more to check and a larger potential scope, but it does not mean nothing can be saved. Call (918) 395-9320 now getting extraction started today is significantly better than waiting another day. Many materials can still be dried if professional drying starts within the first 24 to 48 hours.

Should I call insurance or the restoration company first?

+
If water is still spreading or sitting, call the restoration company first mitigation needs to start immediately. Then open the insurance claim the same day. Both calls should happen today. Your policy requires you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage; starting mitigation is how you meet that requirement.

Can I stay in the house during drying?

+
For clean-water losses (Category 1), usually yes equipment is loud but the home is generally safe to occupy. For sewage backup, floodwater or any Category 3 loss, we recommend staying elsewhere during cleanup. Anyone with respiratory conditions or immune concerns should discuss occupancy with their doctor.

How long until the house is fully dry?

+
Most clean-water losses dry in 3 to 5 days with commercial equipment. Hardwood, plaster, concrete and contaminated water extend the timeline. Equipment stays until daily readings confirm every material has reached its dry target. Timeline guide:

What if I can't find the water source?

+
Call anyway. Thermal imaging can trace moisture behind walls and above ceilings to locate the source without opening the structure. Finding the source is the first step of every mitigation it happens during the initial inspection. See
