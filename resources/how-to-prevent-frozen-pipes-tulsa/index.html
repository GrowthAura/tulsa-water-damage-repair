How to Prevent Frozen Pipes in Tulsa, OK

Tulsa winters are usually mild which is exactly why so many homes have water lines in attics, garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls with minimal insulation. When an Arctic front pushes temperatures well below freezing for a day or more like the February 2021 event that dropped Green Country below zero for multiple days those under-protected lines freeze, expand and split. Thousands of pipes failed across the Tulsa metro during that single event. Here's how to protect your home before, during and after a hard freeze.

Which Pipes Are Most at Risk in Tulsa Homes?

Not all water lines in a home face equal freeze risk. The pipes that fail most during Tulsa hard freezes share one characteristic: they're in spaces that lose heat fastest when outdoor temperatures drop.

Supply lines in attics the highest-risk location. Attic temperatures drop to outdoor levels within hours when the furnace can't keep up. Homes in South Tulsa , Broken Arrow , Owasso , Bixby and Jenks commonly route supply lines through the attic. An attic pipe burst is the most destructive freeze failure because water falls through insulation, ceiling drywall and walls across multiple rooms before anyone notices.

Lines in exterior walls, especially north-facing walls exterior-wall pipe runs have minimal insulation between them and the outdoor temperature. North-facing walls receive no solar warming during the day.

Garage lines garages are typically unheated. Supply lines to laundry rooms, utility sinks and water heaters located in garages are directly exposed to freezing temperatures when the garage door is open or poorly sealed.

Crawlspace lines pier-and-beam homes in Midtown , Brookside , North Tulsa and older Sapulpa have water lines running through crawlspaces that lose heat rapidly during freezes.

Outdoor spigots and sprinkler backflow devices the most exposed fixtures; they're outside the building envelope entirely.

What Should I Do Before Winter to Protect My Pipes?

These prevention steps should happen in the fall before the first hard freeze forecast.

  • Disconnect garden hoses from outdoor spigots a connected hose traps water in the spigot and the supply line behind it, guaranteeing a freeze when temperatures drop. If your spigots have interior shut-off valves, close them and open the spigot to drain the line.
  • Add insulating foam covers to outdoor spigots inexpensive foam covers from any hardware store reduce heat loss at the most exposed point.
  • Insulate exposed supply lines in the attic, garage and crawlspace with foam pipe insulation sleeves. For attic lines in the Tulsa metro, standard thin-wall foam provides basic protection for mild freezes; for extended sub-20°F events, heavier insulation or thermostatically controlled heat tape provides more reliable protection.
  • Seal gaps where pipes pass through exterior walls use spray foam or caulk around penetrations to reduce cold air reaching the pipe.
  • Close crawlspace vents before hard freezes open crawlspace vents allow cold air to flow directly across under-floor pipes.
  • Find your main shut-off valve and confirm it turns you'll need to close it fast if a pipe bursts. Common locations: near the water meter (often in the front yard), in the garage, in a utility closet or near the front foundation wall. In pier-and-beam homes, it may be accessible from the crawlspace.

What Should I Do During a Hard Freeze?

When temperatures are forecast to drop below 20°F or below 28°F for an extended period take these additional steps.

  • Let faucets on exterior walls drip slowly both hot and cold lines. The slight flow keeps water moving through the pipe, which resists freezing. The drip doesn't need to be fast a slow, steady stream is sufficient.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls this allows warm room air to circulate around the pipes under the sink.
  • Keep the thermostat at a steady temperature day and night don't set it back at night during a hard freeze. The furnace needs to maintain interior warmth continuously.
  • Keep the garage door closed if supply lines run through the garage.
  • If you have a pier-and-beam home, check that crawlspace vents are closed and consider running a space heater in the crawlspace near exposed pipes (with appropriate fire safety precautions).

What If I'm Leaving Town During Winter?

Empty homes are the highest-risk category for freeze damage because no one is there to notice a burst pipe or maintain heat.

Option 1 Keep the home heated:

  • Keep the thermostat at 55°F or above never turn off the heat entirely, even for a short trip
  • Leave cabinet doors under exterior-wall sinks open
  • Have a neighbor, friend or property caretaker check the house daily during any hard-freeze forecast
  • Leave your main shut-off valve location and (918) 395-9320 with whoever is checking the house

Option 2 Shut off and drain the system:

  • Close the main water valve
  • Open all faucets (hot and cold) to drain the lines
  • Flush toilets and add RV antifreeze to toilet bowls and traps
  • Turn off the water heater

This option is more work but eliminates the risk of a burst pipe flooding an empty home.

How Do I Tell the Difference Between a Frozen Pipe and a Burst Pipe?

These are two stages of the same problem, and the correct response differs for each.

Frozen pipe (no water or reduced flow from a faucet):
The pipe is blocked by ice but may not have split yet. Shut off the main water valve immediately before it thaws. Open the affected faucet. Warm the area gently with a hair dryer, space heater or warm towels never with an open flame, propane torch or heat gun directly on the pipe (this can cause the pipe to burst unpredictably or start a fire in the wall cavity). Call a plumber to thaw and inspect the line.

Burst pipe (water actively flowing or dripping):
The pipe has split usually the leak starts when the pipe thaws, not while it's frozen. Close the main water valve immediately. Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker panel if it's safe to reach. Photograph the damage. Call (918) 395-9320 for extraction and drying don't wait for the plumber to finish the pipe repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what temperature do Tulsa pipes freeze?

+
Unprotected pipes in attics, garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls are at risk when temperatures stay below about 20°F especially for a day or more. Duration matters as much as the temperature: a single night at 15°F is dangerous, but two days at 25°F can also freeze pipes in poorly insulated spaces.

Does insurance cover frozen pipe damage in Oklahoma?

+
Usually yes frozen pipes are classified as a sudden and accidental loss under most Oklahoma homeowners policies, provided the home was being reasonably heated. Leaving the heat off during winter, or turning it down so low that pipes freeze, can lead to a coverage dispute. See:
